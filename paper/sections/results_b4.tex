H4 is not supported. With UVs preserved on a welded mesh, 23 of 24 assets
reached 6.25\,\% of their faces (about 3,100) in a single pass, with no cracks
(Table~\ref{tab:decimation}). The exception is the asset with the most UV charts
(3,088), which stalled at 3,896 faces after three passes. Charts constrain where
the simplifier may collapse, but at these budgets they rarely stop it. What decided the outcome
was the input representation. MeshLab's glTF loader returns the file's vertex
buffer, in which every UV seam has already split the surface into separate
vertices. Given that, texture-aware simplification treats each seam as an open
boundary and moves its two sides independently: every asset cracked at every
target, with a median of 13,924 open edges and 954 components already at 50\,\%,
and six assets could not reach 6.25\,\% at all
(Figure~\ref{fig:decimation}). Nothing in the filter reports this; the output is a
valid mesh with the requested face count, and it renders. Welding exact
duplicates first, one call, removes every crack at the same face budgets, with
comparable mean error (medians within 0.08\,\% of the diagonal at every target).

This is what happened to the companion. Our pipeline loaded the 497,850-triangle
GLB directly and simplified it in three passes to 82,829 triangles; the result is
the torn mesh of Section~\ref{sec:hygiene}. \input{sections/results_b4_xuanji}
It shipped after a play check in which no one reported a crack; the defect was
found by this audit.
