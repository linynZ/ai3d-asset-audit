\section{Introduction}

Image-to-3D generators now turn a single drawing into a textured mesh in a
minute or two~\cite{zhao2025hunyuan3d20,xiang2025trellis}. For a solo developer
this changes what is affordable: the 15 non-player characters and nine props of
the game studied here each came from one drawing. Generators are usually
evaluated on how their output looks. Engine readiness depends on different
properties: size and orientation, origin, closed and manifold surfaces, memory,
whether the mesh survives simplification, and, once rigged, where the feet are
when it moves.

This paper audits the generated meshes in a completed Unity game, ChronoTraveler,
against those properties, and asks which problems could have been found before
import. The analysis plan was committed before any audit script existed; an
internal fact-check and review of the first draft then led to further
measurements, which we mark as such. The corpus is small (24 assets from one
generator setting, a simplified companion and a rigged boss), so this is a case
study.

Three of the five stated hypotheses were not supported. The raw meshes are
closed and edge-manifold, and their UV layouts fall within the range of the
meshes the game bought. Most of what went wrong came from the pipeline around the
generator rather than from it. The importer reversed the characters' front. The
import path stored each asset's 4096$^2$ base map uncompressed, in most cases
with a CPU copy, and dropped the normal and metallic-roughness maps; these 24 maps are 70\,\% of
the texture data in the build, and none of it left a trace in the commit history.
Our simplification of the companion tore it into 2,433 pieces because the
simplifier (MeshLab) was given the seam-split vertex buffer its glTF loader
returns; welding first leaves every asset crack-free. Two problems did come from the generator:
every asset is scaled to about one metre, and the rigged boss's idle animation
lifts it 0.46--0.68\,m above where its bind pose says its feet are.

Contributions: (1) an audit of generated meshes in a completed game, with
scripts, measurements, commit labels and review reports released; (2) evidence
from this case that engine readiness was decided by conventions, the import
path, simplification input and animation rather than by mesh topology; (3) a
short pre-import checklist (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}), derived here and not
yet validated elsewhere. It extends our earlier audits of AI-generated game
content~\cite{zhao2026featureorleak,zhao2026countingwhat} from text to geometry.
