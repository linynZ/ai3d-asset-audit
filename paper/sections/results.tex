\subsection{Conventions: size from outside, front by the importer}
\label{sec:conventions}

All 24 raw assets carry the same root rotation ($+90^\circ$ about $X$, so the mesh
is stored with its up axis along local $-Z$), all are double-sided, and all use the
same material: one primitive, one material with
\texttt{KHR\_materials\_specular}, and three 4096$^2$ PNG maps (base colour,
normal, metallic-roughness). Every file names Blender's glTF exporter as its
generator. Scale is normalised whatever the subject: the largest extent is
0.895--1.151 units (median 1.082), and glTF units are metres~\cite{khronos2021gltf},
so a soldier, a camel and a stupa all arrive about one metre long. The milestone
falls 0.005 below the plan's 0.9 bound, so H1 as written is not met. The origin
sits on the base of the bounding box in every case and within 0.44--0.58 of its
width and 0.30--0.69 of its depth.

Rendered in glTF coordinates, all 15 NPCs show their faces from $+Z$, the front
the glTF specification prescribes (Figure~\ref{fig:assets}, top). They faced
away from the player in the game because UniGLTF's default conversion reverses
$Z$, and the game's NPC code turns an object's $+Z$ toward the player. The
180\textdegree{} turn in the import tool compensated for the importer, not for
the generator. Real-world size, by contrast, cannot be recovered from the file:
it has to come from the design.

\subsection{Hygiene: closed and manifold; UV layouts like purchased meshes}
\label{sec:hygiene}

H2 is not supported. After welding, all 24 raw assets are closed, edge-manifold
and vertex-manifold, with no degenerate or duplicate triangles; 19 are one
connected component and five have two to four. Every one has self-intersecting
triangles (11--524, median 64). Closed, manifold output is what isosurface
extraction produces~\cite{hunyuan2025hunyuan3d21}, so in hindsight H2 was the
wrong expectation. Other studies describe generated meshes, including Hunyuan3D~2.0
output, as noisy or non-manifold~\cite{wang2025partuv,bhosikar2026faqem}; we
measure after welding, which may explain part of the difference.

Nor do the UV layouts stand out. The raw assets have 703--3,088 UV charts
(14--63 per thousand triangles, median 32), their charts cover 48--68\,\% of the
texture square (median 53\,\%), and seams duplicate 1.31--1.81 vertices per
welded vertex. Our chart counts sit in the range PartUV reports for xatlas on
generated meshes~\cite{wang2025partuv}. The game's 81 non-generated meshes span
similar ranges: the licensed player character has 10 charts per thousand
triangles (its charts are stacked, so coverage is not comparable); the purchased
Temple of Nike pack has 10--44 charts per thousand and covers 36--99\,\%; the
Altar Ruins pack covers a median of 14\,\%; the Egypt pack maps most surfaces to a
colour palette and covers almost nothing. Self-intersections are common in the
non-generated meshes too (54 of 81), and half of them are not closed (41 of 81).
By these measures the generated meshes are as tidy as what the game bought.

The one generated mesh that fails every check is the one we processed. The raw
companion is closed, manifold and one component; the shipped version has 44,316
boundary edges, 1,442 non-manifold vertices, 2,433 components and 9,868
self-intersecting triangles (19 before). Section~\ref{sec:simplification} shows
why.

\subsection{Cost: one uncompressed map per asset, 70\,\% of the build's textures}
\label{sec:cost}

H3 holds for all 24 assets: geometry occupies 1.28--1.60\,MiB as stored, while
three 4096$^2$ maps would take 256\,MiB uncompressed, 64\,MiB in BC7 and 32\,MiB
in BC1 with mips, 20--25 times the geometry even in the smallest
format~\cite{akenine2018rtr4,microsoft_bc7}. It fails for the companion's
high-setting original (497,850 triangles), where the BC7 ratio is 4.3.

The shipped build shows what the pipeline actually did with those maps. Each of
the 24 assets kept one texture, its base colour: the import tool's material
conversion copied the main texture and left the normal and metallic-roughness
slots empty. That one map is stored at 4096$^2$ as uncompressed ARGB32 with a
full mip chain, 85.3\,MiB, because UniGLTF creates embedded textures with
\texttt{LoadImage} rather than through Unity's texture importer; 21 of the 24 are
also flagged CPU-readable, which keeps a second copy in system memory. Together
the 24 maps are 2,048\,MiB, 70\,\% of the 2,944\,MiB of texture data in the
build; the largest texture from any other source is 21.3\,MiB (a 4096$^2$ BC7
map). The boss's and companion's textures, which did go through the texture
importer, were stored block-compressed (DXT1 for colour, DXT5 for normals); at
DXT1 a 4096$^2$ base map would be 10.7\,MiB, at BC7 21.3\,MiB. An AI review of
the project in July flagged the uncompressed maps; nothing was changed
(Section~\ref{sec:lifecycle}). The dropped maps and the CPU copies were never
mentioned.

\subsection{History: few fixes, and most of them ours}
\label{sec:history}

Of 484 non-merge commits, 156 matched the pattern. After review they are 61
\texttt{LEVEL}, 78 \texttt{OTHER}, 7 \texttt{ENGINE} and 10 \texttt{ASSET}. The
pattern was written around asset terms, so the comparison is within matched
commits only. Seven of the ten \texttt{ASSET} commits are tooling written ahead of
need: the NPC import tool (scale to 1.7\,m, turn 180\textdegree, convert
material), its application to each era's models, the boss bake tool, and the
companion's simplification and pivot setup. Three are fixes: the boss hovering
above its platform (Section~\ref{sec:grounding}), and twice the companion lying on
its back after our GLB-to-OBJ conversion dropped the root rotation node.

The first labelling had six \texttt{ASSET} fixes and found H5 not supported
(three of six countable). Review moved the NPC facing fix to \texttt{ENGINE}
(Section~\ref{sec:conventions}) and two ambiguous fixes to \texttt{OTHER}, as
the plan's rule requires: a double 180\textdegree{} turn in our own boss code, and
a scale calibration whose cause mixed the rig and the engine. The boss-hover fix
was also labelled with low confidence, but its cause is not in doubt (E1
reproduces it offline from the asset alone), so it stays \texttt{ASSET}. Two of
the three remaining fixes (the lost rotation node) concern a property counting
would show, and so do nine of the ten \texttt{ASSET} commits, so H5 is supported;
because the relabelling came after the result, we treat this as weak evidence.
Two of the three fixes repaired a step we had added.

\subsection{Grounding: the bind pose misplaces an animated character}
\label{sec:grounding}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/grounding_idle}
\caption{Boss idle loop (1.85\,s) at game scale. Placing the bind-pose feet on
the ground leaves the animated feet 0.46--0.68\,m in the air for the whole loop.
The shipped estimator (minimum of ten samples, dots) lands within about 20\,mm of
the loop's minimum at any starting phase; the remaining 0.22\,m is the idle bob.}
\Description{A line plot of the lowest vertex height over the idle loop, between
0.46 and 0.68 metres, with a dashed line at zero for the bind-pose feet and a
dashed line at 0.46 for the shipped estimator.}
\label{fig:grounding}
\end{figure}

The boss's studio mesh is skinned with its bind-pose feet at the origin, but its
idle animation lifts the whole body (Figure~\ref{fig:grounding}). The bake tool
put the bind-pose feet on the platform, which leaves the animated feet
0.46--0.68\,m above it on every frame. The first run-time fix read the renderer's
bounds, which in the developer's test did not follow the pose; Unity documents
skinned-mesh bounds as precomputed unless updated every
frame~\cite{unity_localbounds}. One sampled frame can land anywhere in the
0.22\,m bob. The shipped estimator, the minimum of ten samples 0.16\,s apart,
covers 1.44\,s of the 1.85\,s loop and in Blender lands within about 20\,mm of the
loop's minimum at any starting phase (median 4\,mm). The remaining 0.22\,m is not
estimator error: one constant offset cannot follow a body that moves up and down,
so it is the floor for any constant, and only per-frame contact handling such as
foot IK~\cite{kovar2002footskate} goes below it. Unity's per-frame ``Feet'' root
mode does this for Humanoid rigs only~\cite{unity_rootmotion}; the generated boss
is imported as a Generic rig.

The constant is also calibrated on one clip. The other three animations dip
lower than idle: relative to the idle-calibrated ground, the lowest frame of the
attack sinks 0.27\,m, the knock-down 0.08\,m and the forward leap 1.19\,m. In the
final build these clips never play (no script fires their triggers), so this is a
latent defect that wiring them up would expose. In the engine the
estimator also needed an empirical 0.30\,m offset, attributed in the commit
record to a scale ambiguity in Unity's \texttt{BakeMesh} for this rig; we did not
reproduce or evaluate it.
