The two extractors found 83 distinct claims about the generated assets, 62 of
them independently. The two judges agreed on 76\,\% of claims before resolution
(Cohen's $\kappa = 0.64$). The 20 disagreements were resolved by the assistant
under two rules: a verdict needs a measurement of the asset the claim is about,
and claims about engine-side readings we did not reproduce are
\texttt{UNTESTABLE}. Fourteen of the 20 went the first judge's way (the first
judge is the assistant itself), and the final verdicts equal the first judge's on
77 of 83 claims, so we also report the second judge's verdicts alone. Forty claims
could not be tested and four concerned non-generated models. Of the 39 testable
claims, 31 were confirmed and eight were contradicted or only partly supported
(20.5\,\%), so H6, set at 20\,\%, is met narrowly; under the second judge's
verdicts alone the share is 31\,\% (16 of 51).

The confirmed claims are mostly readings off a file: triangle counts, texture
sizes, bounding boxes, the root rotation. The wrong ones concern how an asset
relates to the pipeline around it (Table~\ref{tab:claims}). Four blame the
generator for something the pipeline did, three of them being the same $-Z$
explanation (the importer reversed the front) and one the ``hard floor'' (our
script's round limit stopped the simplification). One describes the generator's
output wrongly (the origin is at the base). The other three are a face count at
which simplification had supposedly converged, a pitch with the wrong sign, and
the dismissal of the idle bob. That dismissal rests on an engine reading of
0.04 that we cannot check; offline, the bob alone is 0.22\,m at the game's scale,
the size of the residual hover the developer reported, so the conclusion does not
hold unless the engine scaled the boss differently. The wrong claims split evenly
between the two main models, four each.

Four of the six contradicted claims were never revised (H7). The one that was
copied furthest is the $-Z$ explanation: written into the NPC import tool on
June 23, cited in the boss tool's comment as an ``NPC-pipeline finding'' on
July 9, and repeated as an expectation in the companion's design note on July 15,
written by the other model; it is still in the final build's code.
Of the two revised claims, the pitch was corrected after its effect (a fox
standing on its head) appeared on screen five minutes later, and the face count
36 minutes later, when a commit reported the actual 82,829, although the design
note still says the simplification converged. By kind, two of five testable
causal claims were wrong against three of 14 property claims and two of 14
magnitudes (H8, exploratory); the counts are too small to separate.

Table~\ref{tab:life} is the defect record of the generated assets. H9 holds with
two qualifications. Every defect clearly visible in normal play was fixed except the
idle bob, which was reported once and accepted; each fixing commit came within an
hour of the first commit describing the symptom (the reports themselves were not
recorded). Every defect not visible on screen shipped except one, the boss's
normal-map import type, which the assistant caught in a pre-release code review.
The boss's hover took five commits in 26 minutes: the first had no effect and the
fourth sank the boss 1.5\,m into its platform before the fifth settled it.

One shipped defect had been flagged in writing. On July 15 an external AI review of
the project, committed to the repository, estimated each of the 24 GLBs at about
89\,MB uncompressed in the build and proposed compressing their textures (tr.);
the commit that adopted the review's other fixes the same day did not adopt this
one, no later commit did, and no reason was recorded. The companion's cracks
passed a play check recorded only as ``Play-verified''. The other clips'
predicted sinking is latent: no script in the final build fires the boss's
attack, hit or leap triggers, so only the idle loop ever plays.
