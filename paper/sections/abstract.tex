Image-to-3D generators let a solo developer populate a game with textured
models from single drawings, and their output is judged on how it looks. A
real-time engine judges other things. We audited the 26 generated assets shipped
in a finished Unity game (24 static models from one Hunyuan 3D setting, a
companion we simplified, and a rigged, animated boss) against a plan committed
before any audit script ran. Of five hypotheses, two held and three did not.
The raw meshes are clean: all 24 are
watertight and edge-manifold. Every asset arrives scaled to about one unit,
facing away from the viewer, with three 4096$^2$ maps whose memory is 40--50
times that of the geometry in BC7, and with half of each texture unused. The
defects that cost time were conventions the file does not state, an animation
that lifts the boss 0.46--0.68\,m off the ground at game scale, and our own
processing: simplifying the seam-split vertex buffer a glTF loader returns tore
the companion into 2,433 pieces, while welding first leaves every asset crack-free
at the same budgets. Three of the game's six asset fixes repaired steps we had
added. We release the scripts, measurements and labelled commit history, and
give a short pre-import checklist ordered by what each check would have caught.
