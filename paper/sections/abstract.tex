Image-to-3D generators let a solo developer fill a game with textured models made
from single drawings. We audited the 26 generated meshes of a completed Unity
game (24 static models from one Hunyuan 3D setting, a companion we simplified,
and a rigged, animated boss) against a plan committed before any audit script
ran, then extended the audit after an internal review. Three of five stated
hypotheses were not supported. The raw meshes are closed and edge-manifold, and
their UV layouts fall within the range of the game's purchased meshes. Most
problems came from the pipeline around the generator. The importer reversed the
characters' front, which glTF places at $+Z$. The import path stored each asset's
4096$^2$ base map uncompressed (21 of 24 with a CPU copy) and dropped its normal
and metallic-roughness maps; these 24 maps are 70\,\% of the build's texture data,
and nothing in the commit history recorded it. Simplifying in MeshLab the seam-split
vertex buffer its glTF loader returns tore the companion into 2,433 pieces;
welding first left every asset crack-free at a modest colour cost. Two
problems did come from the generator: every asset is scaled to about one metre,
and the boss's idle animation lifts it 0.46--0.68\,m above its bind-pose feet,
leaving a 0.22\,m bob that no constant offset removes. We release scripts,
measurements, labelled commit history and review reports, and give a short
pre-import checklist derived from the case.
